# Changelog

## 1.11.1

- A game opened from the bottom of the home page now shows its first levels: the level picker used to open scrolled down to the last, locked levels.

## 1.11.0

- New game, Lumi’s sweets, the gentle Kid version of Opencade’s Gourmandises: swap two neighbouring sweets by sliding a finger or with two touches; three alike in a line jump into Lumi’s jar. Every colour also carries a shape (heart, diamond, star, square, round, berlingot), and close colours never share a scene.
- No clock, no move limit, no lives, nothing to lose. A swap that makes no line is a funny try and leaves the board as it was: two sweets bump heads, leapfrog, blush, a friend is tickled, a wrapper crinkles, a sugar cube is too heavy, chocolate is sticky. The board never gets stuck: with no swap left, it mixes itself.
- 32 scenes written by hand, each with a short title said by Lumi: a rainbow sweet that takes a whole colour, wrapped sweets that pop their neighbours, sugar cubes that melt in two, a cherry and a teddy bear to bring down, chocolate that spreads gently, and boards shaped as a heart, a house, an hourglass, a cake, an arch and a rocket. After three tries that make no line, a good swap pulses.
- Erasing the progress in the parents’ space also erases the sweets stars.

## 1.10.0

- New game, Boubou’s bubbles, the gentle Kid version of Opencade’s Bubble Lab: aim with a finger along a long dotted line and throw coloured bubbles at a hanging cluster. Three of a colour that touch fly away as butterflies, and whatever no longer hangs falls gently and grows into flowers. Every colour also carries a shape (heart, diamond, star, square, circle, triangle), and close colours never share a scene.
- No clock, no lives, no ceiling coming down, nothing to lose. A throw that changes nothing is a funny try: boing on the empty ceiling (Boubou scratches his head), a dizzy bubble after many bounces, a friend who waves, a cloud that sneezes, two bubbles that kiss, a face pulled at the wrong colours.
- 32 scenes written by hand, each with a short title said by Lumi or Boubou: a rainbow bubble that goes with every colour, sleepy clouds that float away, a chick, a ladybird and a snail to free from their bubbles, ice bubbles that melt in two throws, a swinging cluster, planks to bounce on. After three throws that change nothing, a golden line shows a good throw.
- Erasing the progress in the parents’ space also erases the bubbles stars.

## 1.9.0

- Lumi’s walk, new levels written as scenes: 52 scenes, each with a short title Lumi says (“A little rabbit is lost”, “The little ones want to see the sea”…) and its own strong moment. Eight sceneries: meadow, autumn, beach, mountain, forest, snow, rain and night.
- New things on the path, all waiting for a touch, never for a clock: a lost rabbit, hedgehog or duckling who follows the troupe to the door, a sleeping bear to tickle, a leaf boat, a flower that lowers a castle drawbridge (even from another floor), a dark cave lit by a jar of fireflies, balloons to float over a tall rock, a bouncy mushroom, a rainbow fork full of treats (the tasty way or straight on), treats to pick into a basket, puddles to splash in and piles of leaves.
- Wrong jobs are now tried for real, and they are funny: a tunnel dug over a ditch ends in a splash, a bridge against a wall folds up like an accordion, a ladder over a ditch becomes a seesaw… twelve little scenes, one for each obstacle and wrong job. Then the little one simply waits again: nothing is lost and they cost no star.
- Harder choices: once two jobs are known, every known job is offered (three or four buttons), not only the right ones. The right one shines and Lumi names it after three tries on the same obstacle, or after about 24 seconds; only that help lowers the stars.
- Progress: the levels changed, so the walk keeps its stars under a new key. As many levels as were open stay open; erasing the progress in the parents’ space erases it too.

## 1.8.0

- New game, Lumi’s walk: Lumi’s five little ones walk from a trapdoor to a rainbow door. In front of a ditch, a wall, soft earth or a step too high, they wait at the edge, never fall; the child touches the one who waits and gives it its job: a bridge, a tunnel, a hole or a ladder. No clock, no lives. A wrong job only makes it shake its head; after two, Lumi names the right one. 52 levels: twelve drawn by hand bring in the jobs one by one, forty generated and checked by a solver go up to four obstacles over three floors, in four sceneries.
- Erasing the progress in the parents’ space also erases the walk stars.

## 1.5.0

Seven kinds of rig take turns (one string, two strings, a slack string, a hook, a bubble that lifts the sweet to Boubou, a bubble that catches it on the way down, four strings), from one gesture to three. ## 1.4.0

- Pestouille’s path: a painted signpost, drawn by the author, now stands at the start of every maze, on the border next to the start square, and points the way of the first step: right on the first two levels, down or up on the later ones (turned a quarter, or mirrored for a path leaving to the left). It sits outside the board, so it never covers a square or Pestouille, and taps on it do nothing.
- The signpost is a small WebP (17 kB) fetched only when a maze opens, never by the home or level screens; until it arrives, and offline before a first visit, a simple drawn signpost shows in its place. Once seen it stays on the device for offline play, kept out of the offline bundle like the painted puzzle scenes.

## 1.3.1

- Tooling: code review now runs on oxlint with its type-aware companion `oxlint-tsgolint`, in place of ESLint and typescript-eslint, which do not accept TypeScript 7. `.oxlintrc.json` keeps the same rules as before, generated from the ESLint configuration it replaces: the type-aware checks (forgotten promises, `any` spreading, unsafe template and `+` operands…) and the engine purity rule (no `Math.random`, `Date` or DOM globals in `src/engines`). Only `no-octal` and `no-dupe-args` are gone, both already syntax errors in ES modules.
- Tooling: TypeScript 7.0.2 (was 6.0.3); `npm run typecheck` is still `tsc --noEmit` and takes about a second. No code change was needed. Dependabot no longer holds back TypeScript major versions.
- CI: browser tests run only for what a change touches (the touched game's specs, the games importing a changed shared module, or one short test per game when the impact cannot be determined), never the whole suite. `scripts/e2e-impact.js` computes the selection, the same locally and in CI; `npm run test:impact` fails when a spec file belongs to no group.
